# Venture Lifecycle and Stage Gates

A lifecycle stage is a decision state, not a decorative status label. Moving forward requires evidence and a recorded decision. The system should reward learning and disciplined capital allocation, not the number of ventures launched.

## Stages

1. **Idea captured** — problem, audience, proposed offer, assumptions and owner are recorded.
2. **Validation** — customer interviews, competitor research, low-cost experiments and willingness-to-pay evidence are collected.
3. **Business case** — value proposition, acquisition plan, unit economics, operating capacity, compliance needs and bounded test budget are documented.
4. **Launch** — a narrow offer is tested with real customers; onboarding, delivery and failure reasons are captured.
5. **Stabilization** — repeatable delivery, service quality, customer feedback, cost visibility and cash controls are demonstrated.
6. **Growth** — expansion is justified by acquisition efficiency, retention, capacity, margins, risk and working capital.
7. **Portfolio review** — choose grow, maintain, repair, pause, restructure, sell or close, and record rationale and review date.

## Required stage-gate record

- Current and proposed stage.
- Decision: pass, hold, revise, pause or stop.
- Evidence items and source URLs/files/record IDs.
- Assumptions and confidence/uncertainty.
- Spend cap and owner/time allocation for the next stage.
- Success criteria and failure/stop criteria.
- Decision maker, timestamp and rationale.
- Dependencies, risks, next review date and exit conditions.

## Validation standards

- Separate observed customer behavior from stated interest.
- Record date, sample size, recruitment method and limitations.
- Do not treat social-media likes or unqualified sign-ups as proof of willingness to pay.
- Use experiments sized to answer one clear question.
- Set a maximum time and spend before the experiment starts.
- Record negative results as useful evidence; never overwrite them to make a venture look healthier.

## Portfolio allocation

Compare ventures using consistent, transparent criteria such as verified demand, repeatability, gross contribution, cash required, downside risk, owner attention, strategic fit and evidence quality. Weights should be configurable and visible. Scores support judgment; they do not automatically authorize investments, layoffs, legal actions, exits or other consequential decisions.

## Stop and pause conditions

Each venture should define conditions that trigger a pause or review: repeated failed validation, insufficient cash runway, unresolved legal or safety obligations, negative unit economics without a credible test plan, delivery capacity constraints, or missing owner attention. A pause is a valid outcome, not a failure to use the system.

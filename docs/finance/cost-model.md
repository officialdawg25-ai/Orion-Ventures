# Operating Cost Model

This is a framework, not a quote. Actual pricing depends on current provider plans, region, usage, billing terms and any applicable taxes.

## Cost categories

1. **Hosting and delivery:** static assets, Worker requests, bandwidth, build minutes and custom domain.
2. **Database and identity:** compute/plan, storage, database egress, backups, authentication email/SMS and branch environments.
3. **Email and messaging:** sending volume, deliverability tooling, verification and abuse protection.
4. **AI:** model/provider usage, embeddings, retrieval, logging and evaluation.
5. **Integrations:** accounting, payments, analytics, maps, file storage and third-party APIs.
6. **Security and operations:** monitoring, vulnerability scanning, backup storage, restore testing and incident response.
7. **People and compliance:** bookkeeping, legal/accounting advice, POPIA processes, support and owner time.
8. **Business execution:** customer research, sales, travel, equipment and working capital per venture.

## Budget controls

- Maintain a dated source for each price and identify free-tier limits.
- Model low, expected and high usage scenarios; include overage and migration cost.
- Separate fixed platform cost from per-venture/per-user/per-transaction cost.
- Estimate the cost of staging and backup before adding paid environments.
- Do not claim a free tier is unlimited or suitable for production without checking its quotas and terms.
- Alert before usage thresholds; avoid automatic paid-plan upgrades without approval.
- Allocate shared costs with a documented method; show unallocated costs rather than hiding them.
- Record cost owner, currency, renewal date, cancellation terms and business purpose.
- Revisit assumptions quarterly or after major usage/provider changes.

## Initial delivery strategy

Build the smallest secure slice first: portfolio membership, venture registry, evidence and stage gates. Avoid costly AI, messaging, payments or complex integrations until the core data model and customer need are proven. Keep production and staging isolated and understand the cost before creating paid resources.
